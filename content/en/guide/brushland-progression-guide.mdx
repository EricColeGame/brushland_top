## Why Brushland Still Earns a Slot

Every two-color deck runs the same early-game test: can you cast a one-drop in one color and a two-drop in another without a land entering tapped and costing you a turn? Brushland passes. It arrives untapped, taps for green or white the moment it hits the battlefield, and asks for a single point of life only when you use a colored mode.

That trade defines the "painland" cycle — ten lands, one for each two-color pair, all built on the same template. Brushland covers green-white, a pairing that shows up in aggressive Selesnya decks, midrange value piles, and casual Commander brews alike.

- **No entry condition.** Nothing to reveal, nothing to pay up front, no "enters tapped unless" clause.
- **Colorless fallback.** The modern printing taps for {C} with no life loss, so it is never a blank land.
- **Budget price.** Typically a few dollars or less, compared with the premium commanded by top-tier duals.
- **Easy to find.** It has been reprinted repeatedly since the mid-1990s, so copies are rarely scarce.

Community reports and player experience generally rank painlands as the strongest budget duals for two-color decks, and Brushland is usually the example players reach for first.

Three details matter more than they look. First, the life loss is **damage**, not a life payment, so damage-prevention effects and "whenever you're dealt damage" triggers apply. Third, Brushland has no land types, so fetch lands cannot search it up.

## The Brushland Progression Guide: Four Stages

A Brushland progression guide is really a guide to sequencing upgrades. Most players buy dual lands in the wrong order — chasing the flashiest option first and fixing consistency last. The four stages below flip that: each one solves the problem you actually have at that point in your collection.

| Stage | Goal | Brushland count | Typical support | Move on when… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Replacement | Stop losing to color screw | 2–3 | Basics plus whatever duals you own | You own better duals, or tempo losses persist |
| 2. Package | Reliable colored mana on turns 1–3 | 3–4 | Check lands, fast lands, modal double-faced lands | Your curve gets heavier in one color |
| 3. Optimization | Untapped mana with less life loss | 2–3 | Shock lands, triomes, surveil lands | Life total starts deciding games |
| 4. Tuning | Match the count to your matchups | 0–4 | Full dual suite, fetch lands | The metagame shifts |

### Stage 1: Replace a Basic, Not a Dual

The first move in any Brushland progression guide is the cheapest one. Swap two or three basic lands for Brushlands and keep everything else intact. You are not trying to build a perfect mana base yet — you are buying the ability to cast a green spell and a white spell on curve. Leave your existing duals alone; cutting them to fit painlands is a lateral move that costs money.

### Stage 2: Build the Package

Once Brushland is in, surround it with lands that cover its weak spots. Check lands and fast lands both enter untapped under conditions Brushland helps satisfy, and modal double-faced lands add color without eating a land slot. This is the stage where most budget decks stop, and honestly, most of them should.

### Stage 3: Optimize

At this point you are adding shock lands and triomes, and Brushland slides from "primary fixing" to "copies five through eight." Its job changes: it is now the land you play when you have already used your premium duals and still need untapped green or white.

### Stage 4: Tune the Count

Finally, tune the number to the matchup. In a fast aggro mirror, four painlands can cost you four or more life across a game — real damage against burn. In a slower Commander pod with 40 starting life, the same four copies are nearly free.

| Deck archetype | Suggested Brushland count | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Selesnya aggro or tempo | 3–4 | You are the beatdown; untapped mana outweighs a point of life |
| Green-white midrange | 2–3 | Enough fixing without stacking pain sources |
| Control | 1–2 | Games go long; life total matters |
| Commander (multiplayer) | 1–2 | 40 life absorbs the cost, and fixing is easy |
| Strict budget two-color | 4 | Cheapest reliable untapped dual available |

## Common Brushland Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Even experienced players misplay painlands. The table below covers the errors that show up most often in deck lists and gameplay.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Treating it as fetchable | No land types means fetch lands cannot find it | Run typed duals if you play fetches |
| Ignoring the life cost in aggro mirrors | Four painlands can equal four-plus life per game | Board in pain-free lands against burn |
| Cutting every basic | Nonbasic hate and ramp effects punish you | Keep three to six basics |
| Stacking too many pain sources | Twelve painlands drains life fast | Cap total pain lands at six to eight |
| Forgetting the colorless mode | You take damage you did not need to take | Tap for {C} when the color is irrelevant |
| Running it as sole fixing in three colors | One land cannot cover three colors | Prioritize lands that produce all your colors |

A quick habit that pays off: count your pain sources the way you count colored sources. If your list runs Brushland plus several other lands that cost life, you are effectively starting some games at 16 or 17 life. Player experience suggests that decks which track this number lose noticeably fewer close races.

Also remember sequencing. Play Brushland on a turn where you need the color immediately; save it for later when a different land covers the early play. Small ordering decisions like that are the difference between a land that costs one life and a land that costs three.

## FAQ

**Is Brushland a good land?**

Yes, within its lane. It provides untapped green or white mana with no condition, which is exactly what two-color decks want on turns one through three. It is not the strongest dual ever printed, but for its price it is one of the most efficient.

**How many copies should I run when following a Brushland progression guide?**

Start with two or three in a midrange shell and go up to four only if you are aggressive or on a strict budget. The right number depends on how much life you can afford to spend, not on how many copies you own.

**Does the damage from Brushland trigger life-loss effects?**

It is damage, not life loss or a life payment, so effects that prevent damage or trigger on damage being dealt to you apply, while effects that care about paying life do not.

**Where can I verify rules and legality before buying?**

Use an official card database for oracle text and format legality, and a price aggregator for current listings. Legality shifts with rotation, so confirm before you commit to a playset.

**What is the fastest way to apply a Brushland progression guide on a tight budget?**

Buy the playset first, then add check lands and fast lands before anything expensive. That order gives you the largest consistency gain per dollar and keeps your mana base functional while you save for premium duals.
