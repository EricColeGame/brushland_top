Brushland does not use a conventional tech tree or a ticking clock. Progression comes from the landscape itself: better terrain creates better buildings, new buildings attract villagers, and fulfilled requests open the way to larger districts. This guide breaks that loop into clear stages so you always know what to improve next.

## Stage 1: Establish the First Settlement

Begin with a wide patch of grassland and a simple road connection. Add a dock on open water to invite the first villagers, then watch where homes form. Do not overbuild yet. Your goal is to learn how the terrain resolves and to identify the flattest, most connected area for a village core.

Keep a little space between the first houses. Buildings need room to change, and a crowded start makes later expansion harder.

## Stage 2: Stabilize Food, Work, and Access

Once villagers arrive, give them reasons to stay. Fields support windmills and working landscapes, roads connect homes to the center, and docks keep new boats coming. Answer early requests for bridges or access before adding decorative terrain.

This is also the point to improve readability. A district should have a clear entrance, a recognizable center, and a natural edge. Rivers, rock lines, and mountain ridges can provide those boundaries without walls.

## Stage 3: Expand Into New Districts

When the first village feels stable, extend the road spine into a second area. Use a different terrain idea for each district: a riverside quarter, a farming belt, or a hillside cluster. Keep the road connection obvious so the settlement reads as one kingdom rather than isolated patches.

New villagers will bring new requests. Treat those requests as a progression checklist. They point toward the next building type, the next connection, or the next view worth protecting.

## Stage 4: Build Toward Landmarks

Larger buildings emerge when the layout supports them. A house ringed by road can become a church, while sustained growth can raise a castle. Plan a generous open area for these landmarks and keep the surrounding roads simple enough that the building's shape remains readable.

At this stage, color looks become useful. Try a palette on the finished district, compare the mood, and keep the version that makes the roads and terrain easiest to read.

## Stage 5: Refine and Share

Progression does not end with the biggest building. Refine edges, open a view, or replace a crowded cluster with a cleaner route. When the kingdom feels complete, fold it into a text code or use Steam Workshop tools to share it. A finished build is also a starting point for the next one.
