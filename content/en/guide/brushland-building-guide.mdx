## Brushland at a Glance: What the Card Actually Does

Two-color decks live and die by their mana base, and few lands solve that problem as cleanly — or as affordably — as Brushland. This Brushland building guide walks you through exactly what the card does, when paying life is worth it, and how many copies a green-white deck actually wants. Get the mana right and your deck gets a full turn faster; get it wrong and you lose games before you cast your second spell.

Before you can build with it, you need to know what you're buying. Brushland is a Land — not a creature, not an artifact, and importantly, not a land with basic land types. That last detail shapes nearly every deck-building decision in this Brushland building guide.

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Card type | Land |
| Ability one | {T}: Add {C} (colorless mana, no life lost) |
| Ability two | {T}: Add {G} or {W}; Brushland deals 1 damage to you |
| Rarity | Rare |
| Featured printing | The Brothers' War #259, illustrated by Thomas Stoop |
| Other printings | Tales of Middle-earth Commander and Magic Online promos, among others |
| Typical market price | Roughly $1.50–$1.75 for a nonfoil copy |

The Brothers' War flavor text frames the card nicely: Urza fortified a position every thirty miles along the road to Tomakul, building a trench network the two brothers would trade back and forth for decades. For a land whose whole job is holding contested ground between two colors, that's about as on-theme as it gets.

## Why the Pain Land Earns a Slot

Players call this cycle "pain lands" because the colored mode hurts. There's no enters-the-battlefield-tapped clause and no condition to satisfy — Brushland is always ready, and the only question is whether you're willing to shave a life point for the color you need.

That trade is usually excellent in the first three turns and increasingly poor afterward. A one-drop on turn one or a two-drop on turn two changes the shape of a game; a random four-drop on turn six does not, and by then you should be using the colorless mode or a different land entirely.

| Game turn | Typical use | Life paid | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turn 1 | Green one-drop | 1 | Almost always worth it |
| Turn 2 | White two-drop | 1 | Usually worth it |
| Turn 3 | Double-pip spell | 1 | Matchup dependent |
| Turn 5+ | Filler spell | 1 | Skip it — use {C} instead |
| Any turn | Spell you can't cast otherwise | 1 | Always worth it |

One subtle but real advantage: because Brushland *deals damage* rather than simply causing life payment, it interacts differently with the rest of the game. Damage-prevention effects can blank it, and it can trigger abilities that care about being dealt damage. Community reports note that this occasionally matters in Commander pods built around damage triggers.

## Brushland Building Guide: Sizing the Mana Base

Most players get this wrong in the same direction — they either jam four copies into a deck that only splashes white, or they cut it entirely for a tapland that costs them tempo every game. Use the sequence below to find the right number.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Count your colored pips by turn | Tells you how badly you need untapped duals early |
| 2 | Count how many untapped sources you need on turns 1–3 | This is the real demand curve |
| 3 | Start with 2–3 copies | A safe baseline for most two-color decks |
| 4 | Add a fourth only if you have no better untapped dual | Pain lands are a floor, not a ceiling |
| 5 | Cap all pain lands combined at 4–6 | Life loss stacks across the whole cycle |
| 6 | Play 20+ games and track your life total | Data beats theory every time |

Two structural facts should guide the count. First, Brushland has no basic land types, so fetch lands cannot find it — a mana base built around fetching will get less value from it than a straightforward one. Second, the colorless mode is genuinely useful: decks running spells with generic or colorless requirements can use Brushland without ever paying life, which effectively makes it a free untapped land in those slots.

If your deck needs double-green on turn two *and* double-white on turn three, a single pain land won't rescue that curve. Fix the pip requirements first, then use Brushland to smooth the gaps.

## Brushland Building Guide: Which Decks Actually Want It

Not every green-white deck wants the same count. The table below reflects common archetypes and typical starting points; adjust based on your local metagame and how aggressive your curve is.

| Archetype | Suggested copies | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Selesnya aggro or tokens | 3–4 | Needs untapped green-white on turns 1–3 every game |
| GW midrange | 2–3 | Slower curve, better duals often available |
| GW enchantment or value shells | 2 | Color requirements are usually single-pip |
| Mono-green with a white splash | 1–2 | Splash cards are few and rarely early |
| Two-color Commander deck | 1 (singleton format) | 40 starting life makes the damage trivial |
| Heavy double-pip control | 0–2 | Color-intensive costs punish colorless sources |

In Commander specifically, Brushland is one of the better budget duals available. You start at 40 life, games run long, and the one damage per activation is close to irrelevant — while entering untapped matters enormously in a format where a tapped land can cost you a whole turn cycle.

For a two-color Standard or Modern-style build, the calculus flips: every point of life is real, so the land should be doing genuine work in your opening turns or it shouldn't be in the deck.

## Brushland vs. Other Green-White Lands

Comparison shopping is where most mana bases get better. Here's how the pain land stacks up against the other common options, without pretending it wins every category.

| Land | Enters untapped? | Life cost | Fetchable | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brushland | Always | 1 per colored use | No | Budget bases, early tempo, Commander |
| Shock-style dual (e.g., Temple Garden) | Yes, if you pay | 2 once | Yes (has land types) | Fetch-heavy, competitive bases |
| Check-style dual (e.g., Sunpetal Grove) | Conditional | 0 | No | Decks already heavy in one color |
| Fast-style dual (e.g., Razorverge Thicket) | Conditional on land count | 0 | No | Aggressive low-curve decks |
| Basic Forest or Plains | Always | 0 | Yes | Consistency, Blood Moon insurance |

The pattern is simple: Brushland is the most reliable unconditional untapped dual in the budget tier, and it pays for that reliability in life rather than in dollars. A full playset costs less than many single premium duals, which is exactly why so many budget decklists lean on it.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Running four copies in a light splash.** If only four cards in your deck need white mana, you're paying life for a color you rarely use.
- **Ignoring the colorless mode.** Plenty of decks can cast their early spells off {C} and save the damage for the turn it actually matters.
- **Forgetting it can't be fetched.** If your mana base relies on fetch lands, Brushland doesn't help you hit your colors.
- **Stacking pain lands without counting.** Four Brushlands plus four other pain lands adds up to six or more life per game, which is a full card against aggressive decks.
- **Assuming it fixes double pips.** One Brushland produces one mana. It does not solve a demanding two-color curve on its own.

A good habit: after each session, note how much life you paid to lands versus how much tempo you gained. If the number is consistently above four, trim a copy. If you never paid life at all, you probably weren't using the card and can cut it for something with more upside.

## FAQ

**How many copies of Brushland should I run in a two-color deck?**
Start with two or three and adjust from there. Aggressive decks that need untapped green and white mana on turns one through three can justify four, while slower decks with access to premium duals usually want two at most.

**Does the one damage matter in Commander?**
Rarely. With 40 starting life and long games, the damage is negligible, which makes Brushland one of the strongest budget duals in the format. The bigger win is that it always enters untapped.

**Can fetch lands find Brushland?**
No. Brushland has no basic land types, so effects that search for a Forest or Plains cannot find it. Build accordingly if your mana base leans on fetching.

**Is Brushland worth it on a budget?**
For most green-white decks, yes. This Brushland building guide recommends it as a default inclusion in budget two-color bases because it reliably enters untapped, costs very little, and can even produce colorless mana without any life payment.
