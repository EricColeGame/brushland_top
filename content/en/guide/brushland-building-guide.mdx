Building in Brushland starts before you place a single home. The game grows structures from the terrain and from the relationships between neighboring tiles, so a good layout begins with landforms, roads, and open space. This guide walks through a reliable building sequence, from the first painted meadow to a kingdom large enough for churches and castles.

## Paint the Land Before the Village

Start with a broad, readable base. Grassland is the main canvas for homes, so paint a generous area and keep it open. Add rock and mountain shapes around the edges to frame the settlement, then carve rivers and waterfalls before committing to roads. Water follows elevation, and a river that cuts through the middle of a future district will force a redesign later.

A useful rule is to separate the map into three zones: a flat village core, a working belt for fields and docks, and a scenic edge of mountains or forest. You do not need to fill every zone immediately. Leaving room is part of the plan.

## Use Roads as Structure

Roads connect doors and organize movement, but they also shape what buildings become. A house surrounded by road can evolve into a church, while a home with access to fields may support a windmill. Build a simple road spine through the village core first, then add branches toward future housing clusters.

Avoid wrapping every building in road too early. Dense loops can look busy and leave no room for expansion. Start with clear routes, check how the settlement grows, and add detail where the game actually places buildings.

## Give Homes Room to Change

Cottages need space to grow into larger structures. Cluster homes with small gaps between them rather than packing them tightly. Keep fields near the homes that need them, and reserve a larger open area for a future church, market, or castle.

Docks belong on open water and should connect back to the road network. They bring boats, new villagers, and fresh requests, so treat a dock as the start of a new district rather than a decorative endpoint.

## Build in Passes

The strongest Brushland layouts are refined in small passes. Paint the broad shapes, add the main road, place the first housing cluster, then observe. Repaint edges, widen a route, or move a field after you see how the village responds. This iterative approach is faster than trying to solve the whole map in one pass.
