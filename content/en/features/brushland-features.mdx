Brushland is a cozy sandbox kingdom builder built around a simple promise: paint the landscape, then watch a fairytale settlement emerge from it. The confirmed feature set is compact but unusually interconnected. Terrain affects buildings, buildings affect villagers, and the finished kingdom can be recolored or shared. Here is how those pieces fit together.

## Terrain Painting Is the Core Mechanic

You do not place finished buildings from a menu. Instead, you paint tiles: grass, rock, water, roads, and other landscape types. The game reads how those tiles meet and resolves them into forms such as mountains, rivers, waterfalls, fields, and homes.

That adjacency rule is what makes the system feel like a sandbox rather than a level editor. A road can change what a house becomes. A field can support a windmill. A mountain edge can frame a district and give the skyline depth. Planning the terrain first is therefore more important than decorating afterward.

## Villages Grow From Your Layout

Villagers arrive by boat and move into the buildings that form from the painted land. They fish, work, and go about daily routines, making the kingdom feel inhabited rather than static. Requests give the player a gentle direction: build a bridge, create work, or improve a view.

Brushland is designed without a clock and without a failure state. There is no countdown forcing a decision and no permanent loss condition. You can experiment, repaint an area, and expand at your own pace.

## Color Looks Change the Mood

The announced 42 color looks let you reskin a completed kingdom without changing its layout. A palette can shift the same landscape from bright storybook greens to a darker, more atmospheric interpretation. This is a presentation feature, but it adds a lot of replay value for players who enjoy revisiting a finished build.

## Sharing and Creation Tools

Finished kingdoms can be folded into a short text code and sent to another player. The included level editor and Steam Workshop support extend that idea to custom maps and scenarios. Steam Cloud is also listed, so kingdoms can remain available across machines.

## What to Expect at Launch

The announced plan is a demo in Q1 2027 and a full launch in Q2 2027 for Windows, macOS, Linux, and Steam Deck. Details can change, so check the [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5163450/Brushland/) before planning around a date.
