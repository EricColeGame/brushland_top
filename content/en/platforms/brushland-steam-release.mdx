## How Brushland Plays: Paint Tiles, Grow a Kingdom

Brushland is a fairytale kingdom-builder built on a single idea: every tile you paint changes what happens next. Paint rock on high ground and mountains rise and catch snow. Paint water near a cliff and rivers find their own way down as waterfalls. Each tile checks its neighbors and decides what to become, which means the map can surprise the person who drew it.

The adjacency logic is where the game gets its personality. A lonely house ringed by road turns into a church with a belfry and spire. A house surrounded by fields becomes a windmill with sails that actually turn. Keep painting and the settlement starts getting ideas above its station, eventually growing a castle with round towers in pointy hats.

| What You Paint | What the Kingdom Does |
| --- | --- |
| Rock on elevated ground | Mountains rise and collect snow |
| Water beside a cliff | River tips over the edge as a waterfall |
| Road wrapped around one house | House becomes a church with belfry and spire |
| Fields surrounding a house | House becomes a working windmill |
| Dock on open sea | Boats arrive with new villagers and their suitcases |
| Sustained building and roads | Hamlet grows into a castle-topped kingdom |

Once a dock touches open water, boats start arriving. Villagers walk the roads to their new front doors, smoke rises from chimneys, and they announce their plans in short, dry little lines — "Gone fishing," "Milling merrily," "Doorstep dawdling." One of them carries a sheep and offers no explanation. Later they start asking for things: a bridge here, a mine there, work to do. Answering those requests is how a handful of tiles becomes a country.

The pressure valve is deliberately missing. There is no clock and no failure state. The worst outcome is painting a lake under a crowd, at which point the villagers cartwheel away yelling "Falling! Mercy!" and swim off to find a new home — and even that can be undone. When you are finished, a kingdom can try on 42 color looks and then be folded into a short text code you can send to a friend like a letter.
